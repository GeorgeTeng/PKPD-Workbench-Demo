Terminal phase and plasma profiles

Which late points set the terminal half-life in each animal?

In all 6 horses after IV dosing the late points fall on a straight line on the semi-log plot, with a terminal half-life of about 1.4 h. Model elimination as one linear clearance from the central compartment, with between-animal variability on clearance. Only one dose level per route was given, so dose-dependent (nonlinear) clearance cannot be tested. There are no urine or metabolite data, so only total clearance can be estimated. After IM dosing the late decline is much slower than after IV dosing (half-life about 17 h), so absorption sets it there; anchor elimination on the IV data. As a check, the slowest phase of the fitted model should decline with a half-life of about 1.4 h.

What does the plasma window tell us about clearance?

After IV dosing, dose divided by the observed exposure is similar across all 6 horses. Start clearance at or below about 0.67 L/h/kg; the samples miss part of the curve, so this is an upper estimate.

Numbers behind this

DBG03_Horse_IV (I001, individual): the observed AUC over 0.1–8.42 h was 16.6 mg·h/L (AUC/dose 1.35 h·kg/L); the dose-to-observed-AUC ratio was 0.74 L/h/kg, based on parent Compound_C (selective assay). See the evidence table for 5 additional profiles.

Leaving out the area before and after the window puts this value on the high side of D/AUC(0,∞) when the trapezoid is accurate. Integration error is not bounded here, so the net direction for this profile is unresolved (E0.6). Estimating CL requires verified parent-drug exposure over the full profile.
