Q1

Systemic disposition

Answerable

Do the measured systemic concentrations support a parent-disposition test?

All 6 horses show a fast early decline followed by a slower terminal phase on the semi-log plot. Use at least two compartments and start with a two-compartment model (central + one peripheral), because the early points bend away from a single straight line. In 5 of 6 horses the decline still bends after the early points are dropped, so a three-compartment model is a candidate; test it against two.

Numbers behind this

Early decline followed by a slower decline in 6 of 6 profiles.

Main view I001: R = 5.34, z = 13.7, the reading holds without the first or the last point; first sample 0.0167 h after input ended. Numerical check, not a formal test (no sourced error range): one straight line is contradicted in 6 of 6 profiles at the ±5 % reading tolerance and in 6 once every point is widened to the noise setting (±16 %). Individual profiles. Compare one compartment with reversible peripheral exchange against the early and late observations.

Vc bound

Central volume

Answerable

How tightly can early IV observations bound the initial central volume?

The first sample after dosing caps the central volume in all 6 horses, and the cap is similar across animals. Start the central volume at or below about 0.14 L/kg.

Numbers behind this

Upper bounds on Vc, one per animal: median 0.144 L/kg, range 0.138–0.215 L/kg (6 animals, all at end of infusion + 0.498 min (0.0416–0.0916 h after the start of dosing) to end of infusion + 2 min (0.0667–0.117 h after the start of dosing)).

Each bound is D/C(t) at that animal's highest quantified sample at or after input end and holds for that animal only. Every animal lies below its own bound, so the median animal's Vc is at or below the median bound; the smallest bound (0.138 L/kg, I002) does not limit the others. At end of infusion + 0.498 min (0.0416–0.0916 h after the start of dosing) to end of infusion + 2 min (0.0667–0.117 h after the start of dosing) the bounds differ 1.6-fold between animals given the same dose. Plasma-volume check: not assessable. Keep in mind: sample within 5 min of the end of dosing (mixing may be incomplete); early sample with the sampling site not arterial or not reported. As a fit constraint: 0 of 6 bounds are formal (joint lower concentration limits over the candidate samples); the others are sensitivity results: Insufficient evidence: no source precision, source assay precision or replicates missing; no supported upper limit on the delivered dose; the recorded per-animal dose is used; the limiting sample is within 5 minutes of the end of input (mixing unresolved). Use the median bound to check a typical fitted Vc and each animal's bound for its own estimate. Q and peripheral volume require information on exchange over time.

Missing: no sourced plasma-volume value for Horse in the run configuration

Working hypothesis

Review pending

Taken together, what should a model check next about distribution?

All 6 horses show a fast early decline followed by a slower terminal phase on the semi-log plot. Use at least two compartments: start with a two-compartment model with between-animal variability on clearance and the central volume, and choose the terminal window by eye on each profile. In 5 of 6 horses the decline still bends after the early points are dropped, so a three-compartment model is a candidate; test it against two. A one-compartment model is kept only as the baseline fit, because the data do not formally rule it out.

Numbers behind this

Decline shape: The IV decline slows in 6 of 6 profiles (each holds without the first or the last point), so at least two compartments are supported; numerically, one straight line is contradicted in 6 of 6 at the noise setting (not a formal test).

Terminal window: A candidate start for an IV terminal window is 2 h; confirm against the profile (item 18).

Initial volume: A typical initial Vc should start at or below about 0.144 L/kg (median of 6 per-animal bounds, range 0.138–0.215 L/kg).

Between animals: At the same dose, concentrations differ 1.2-fold at end of infusion + 1 min (0.05–0.1 h after the start of dosing) (5 animals) and 1.7-fold at end of infusion + 4.12 h (4.15–4.2 h after the start of dosing) (6 animals); the area from end of infusion + 4 min (0.1–0.15 h after the start of dosing) to end of infusion + 4.12 h (4.15–4.2 h after the start of dosing) differs 1.4-fold (CV 14 %); allow between-animal variability on clearance and on the initial volume and let the fit show which is needed.

Recommended model: at least two compartments; start with two (central + one peripheral). The simpler model (one compartment, linear, IV input as dosed) is fitted only as a baseline, to confirm that the recommended model fits better. A three-compartment model is a candidate where the decline still bends after the early points; test it against two.

Missing: matched total plasma and applicable fu,p, or measured unbound plasma; no sourced plasma-volume value for Horse in the run configuration

What this rests on

Rests on: item 24 split-half reading, item 25 bound, observed concentrations at shared times. Record level: individual. Target binding: not stated by the source, so target-mediated disposition is not considered.
