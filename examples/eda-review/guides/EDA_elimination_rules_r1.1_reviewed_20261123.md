## Main question

**How is the parent drug eliminated in this species, and what do the measurements reveal about its metabolism?**

For each study and species, identify where unchanged parent or its transformation products were recovered. Use plasma and urine measurements to assess whether elimination changes with dose or repeated dosing.

## Candidate models and what they mean

**Elimination models for comparison.** Clearance (CL) relates the rate at which parent drug is eliminated to its concentration on a stated plasma or blood basis. Under linear, time-invariant pharmacokinetics, the full area under the concentration–time curve (AUC) after a single dose satisfies $AUC_{0-\infty}=FD/CL$, where $D$ is the administered dose and $F$ is systemic bioavailability ($F=1$ for intravenous dosing). An extravascular profile generally identifies apparent clearance $CL/F$. These relationships refer to the parent drug and the complete exposure window.

The first three rows describe removal rates. The next three cover routes and transformation. Protein binding can modify the observed exposure and filtration, and enterohepatic recycling can return drug to the circulation. Several processes can contribute to the same profile.

| Elimination model or pathway | Structure and parameters | What would distinguish it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Linear (first-order) elimination** | Elimination rate $=CL\,C$. Clearance is constant over the range studied. A one- or multicompartment model can have linear elimination. After extravascular dosing with unknown $F$, the apparent clearance is $CL/F$. | Compare dose-normalized IV profiles and AUC/dose over the same window. Overlap supports trying a linear model over that range. |
| **Capacity-limited elimination** | Total rate $=CL_{lin}C+V_{\max}C/(K_m+C)$. The saturable pathway reaches half its maximum rate $V_{\max}$ at $C=K_m$. Set $CL_{lin}=0$ for that pathway alone. | A supported rise in IV AUC/dose warrants checking delivery, sampling, binding and assay. Well below $K_m$, observations chiefly inform $V_{\max}/K_m$. A separate linear clearance needs data that distinguish the two contributions. |
| **Time-dependent clearance** | For a linear pathway whose activity changes over time, rate $=CL(t)\,C$. Kidney function or enzyme activity may change during treatment. The model can use occasion-specific clearance or enzyme turnover. | Compare matched sampling times on the same regimen. Two occasions can show a difference. Estimating its time course needs an informative dosing and sampling history. |
| **Renal excretion of unchanged parent** | Filtration and secretion deliver parent to tubular fluid. Reabsorption and renal loss reduce unchanged urinary recovery. For constant clearances, $CL=CL_R+CL_{nr}$ and the ultimate unchanged urinary fraction of systemic parent is $f_e=CL_R/CL$. | Compare unchanged urinary amount with plasma exposure over the same interval. For freely filtered unbound drug with stable binding and GFR, filtration alone predicts $CL_R=f_{u,p}\,GFR$. |
| **Conversion of parent to metabolites** | In a simple one-compartment metabolite model, the change in metabolite amount per unit time equals formation minus elimination on a common molar basis. Product recovery and assay kinetics are observable on their own bases. | Check product identity, recovery, stability and matched controls. Oral dosing allows formation before or after systemic entry. Estimating a formation fraction and metabolite clearance separately requires a design that distinguishes them. |
| **Biliary excretion of unchanged parent** | The liver delivers parent into bile. Record the amount collected or its fraction of a stated dose, with the interval and extent of external drainage. | A selective assay in directly collected bile supports this route. Faecal label alone leaves both the chemical species and its route into the gut unresolved. |
| **Plasma protein binding** | Binding changes the unbound fraction and can affect total plasma concentrations and the unbound amount available for filtration. | Compare total and unbound concentrations on the same arms, times and dose basis. Check fraction unbound across the concentrations observed. |
| **Enterohepatic recycling** | Parent or conjugate enters the gut via bile, allowing parent to be reabsorbed. | Bile measurements establish delivery via bile. Matching intact and diverted profiles or tracing parent after donor-bile transfer helps assess re-entry. |

Here $f_{u,p}$ is the unbound fraction in plasma and GFR is the glomerular filtration rate. For a urine interval $[a,b]$, the interval estimate is $CL_R=\Delta A_e/AUC_p(a,b)$. Use unchanged urinary parent amount and total parent plasma exposure over the same interval. A recovery percentage, an excretion rate (amount/time) and a renal clearance (volume/time) answer different questions.

With constant clearances, the remainder after unchanged urinary clearance is $CL_{nr}=CL(1-f_e)$. It can include metabolism, net biliary loss after any return to systemic parent, and loss of parent in the kidney before it appears in urine. Gross bile collected during external drainage describes that design. This remainder alone cannot quantify metabolism. With constant clearance fractions after a single extravascular dose, **complete** unchanged urinary recovery divided by administered dose equals $F f_e$ when urinary parent comes from circulating parent. A finite collection gives the recovery through its endpoint. Systemic $f_e$ also requires independently known $F$ or an appropriate IV recovery study.

| What needs distinguishing? | Candidate explanations | What in the observations helps? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Exposure changes with dose** | Capacity-limited elimination, concentration-dependent binding, altered delivery or distribution. Extravascular dosing also allows a change in systemic input. | Compare AUC/dose over a common window and normalized concentrations at matched times. Check delivered dose, assay and binding across the observed range. |
| **A later dose gives a different profile** | Ordinary accumulation, altered clearance or a change in the amount entering circulation. | Compare the drug-free first occasion with later occasions at the same dose, interval and sampling times. A supported fall challenges identical input with linear time-invariant disposition. |
| **Little parent was recovered in urine** | A small renal contribution, incomplete collection or low systemic availability after extravascular dosing. | A lower urinary amount divided by the upper dose gives a lower recovery limit. Calling the renal route minor requires an upper limit on eventual recovery. |
| **A product appears in excreta** | Transformation in vivo, chemical degradation or a change after collection. | Read the identification method, product amount and recovery denominator alongside handling, stability and incubation controls. Attribution to an enzyme or transporter needs independent evidence. |

## What can we tell from the figures?

Start with the measurements available in the study. Use questions 1–3 for plasma exposure, dose comparisons or matched dosing occasions. Use question 4 for urine, question 5 for identified products or incubations, and question 6 for direct bile or route recovery. Use question 7 for protein binding and question 8 for evidence of biliary return to circulation. A urine, product or bile result can stand on its own.

**1. How much systemic exposure did the study observe?** Figure 1 shows when the samples were taken. Table 1 gives exposure over that window. For an isolated known dose with at least three quantified postdose observations and positive area, report AUC and AUC per dose over those endpoints, the assay and the analysis unit. Use actual times, linear trapezoids on rising or flat segments and logarithmic trapezoids on positive falling segments under the shared method. A predose IV value does not stand for the immediate post-bolus concentration. Stop integration at an unexplained gap, an interior below-limit sample or an unsupported endpoint. Report only windows with adequate continuous sampling support. Both IV and extravascular profiles can fully answer this question when sampling is adequate. An area calculated from a group curve describes that curve. Partial-window AUC measures observed exposure and does not identify whole-profile clearance.

**2. Are the dose comparisons consistent with linear elimination, or is there evidence to investigate saturation?** Compare AUC/dose over the same window at all doses (Figure 3). On Figure 1 inspect the normalized profiles, and on Figure 2 inspect their ratios at matched times. For comparable IV arms with dose-scaled input profiles, overlap is consistent with shared linear disposition over the observed window. A supported rise in IV AUC/dose makes saturation a candidate. Check whether organ function or perfusion also changed. Check whether the matched-time ratio remains at a similar level or changes during the decline. A partial-window contrast can also reflect redistribution across time. Check delivered dose, sampling coverage, assay and binding (Table 1 if measured). For extravascular dosing, bioavailability and input timing can also change window exposure. Report the prespecified contrast, uncertainty and any multiplicity adjustment. Identify confounding when each dose corresponds to a different, unreplicated animal or condition.

**3. Can carryover from earlier doses explain the repeated-dose profile?** Compare the drug-free first occasion with later occasions at the same dose, interval and sampling times (Figures 1 and 2). Use the same exposure window and verify route, formulation, dose delivery and baseline. If route or formulation also changed, report a regimen contrast. For within-animal inference, verify the pairing and account for it in the uncertainty. Higher exposure is compatible with accumulation. When the first-dose profile covers the required lags, compare the later observations with the sum of dose-aligned residual contributions under the actual dosing history and the same-input, linear reference model. A prediction extending beyond measured lags needs a supported tail estimate. Interpret the full dosing-interval exposure separately from a partial window. A supported fall under matched dosing raises increased clearance and reduced input as alternatives. Check changes in the animals studied and in the assay. Read urine by occasion on Figure 4 if available. Two occasions describe a contrast. If pharmacokinetic samples cover only the first dose, the later response is unknown even when repeat dosing continued in the pharmacokinetic study or was assessed separately for efficacy.

**4. How much parent was excreted unchanged in urine, and can renal clearance be assessed?** Read recovery through the last observed collection on Figure 4a or use Table 1 for a single endpoint. Give the denominator and collection coverage. A supported lower bound of at least 50% of the administered dose establishes that at least half was recovered unchanged. A small percentage after extravascular dosing leaves the fraction of systemic parent excreted in urine open unless bioavailability and recovery coverage support a bound. For two or more contiguous bins with positive cumulative recovery, the project labels the last increment small if it contributes less than 5% of observed recovery and its bin is at least as long as the preceding one. This describes the observed endpoint. Use Figure 4b for interval rates when needed. Retain a reported dose percentage on its original basis. Converting it to an amount for renal clearance requires the delivered dose in compatible units.

For interval renal clearance, match urinary parent amount to total plasma exposure, preferably within the same animal (Figure 4c or Table 1). Matched parallel cohorts can yield a ratio of group quantities. State the cohort and aggregation for each quantity. A ratio of group means and the mean of individual clearance estimates answer different questions. To compare that ratio with filtration, weight each animal’s predicted filtration clearance by its plasma exposure. Separate group means alone generally leave that prediction unknown. With free filtration, stable binding and GFR, negligible urinary delay and no secretion, reabsorption or renal loss, $CL_R=f_{u,p}\,GFR$. A clearance lower bound above the upper filtration limit supports investigating net secretion. An upper bound below the lower filtration limit warrants checking reabsorption, renal loss, collection and stability. Overlapping eligible two-sided ranges leave filtration compatible. A one-sided bound in the wrong direction cannot decide the comparison.

With directly measured unbound plasma exposure and constant GFR, compare $\Delta A_e$ with $GFR\times AUC_u(a,b)$ over the same interval. Report both quantities and their uncertainty in amount units in Table 1. Directly measured unbound exposure captures changes in binding. For a group, average the predicted filtered amounts using compatible animals and weights. Across comparable intervals, falling renal clearance with rising mean plasma concentration can motivate testing saturable secretion. Rising renal clearance can motivate testing saturable reabsorption. Check binding, kidney function, collection and handling. The same estimated AUC enters the concentration and clearance calculations in opposite directions. Inspect the original urine amounts and carry the same AUC uncertainty through both calculations before interpreting a trend.

**5. Which products were identified, how much was recovered, and what supports their formation?** Use Table 3 for the identity and share of each component. Use Table 2 for the recovery of the same excretum. Match cohort or pool, collection window, label and quantitative basis. Using fractions on a 0–1 scale, $p_{\rm product,dose}=p_{\rm product,excretum}\,p_{\rm excretum,dose}$. The two factors are the product’s share of all label recovered in that excretum and the excretum’s share of the administered label. For a chromatogram area share, establish extraction and profiling coverage and a quantitative link between detector response and radiolabel. Without that link, retain the reported peak-area percentage. Calculate the dose share within each animal before averaging, or use pooled amounts with a common denominator. Multiplying two separately averaged percentages can give a different result. When combining compatible non-overlapping collections, sum the recovered amounts before calculating the percentage. Use stability and handling records to assess formation after collection. For masses of different molecules, use supported molecular weights and stoichiometry before adding parent equivalents. A product recovered in one specimen does not establish the whole-body fraction metabolized.

If the study reports incubations, read the matched controls and assay conditions in Table 4. Use Figure 5 for a measured time course or comparable endpoints across species. Compare the active preparation with its matched no-enzyme or inactivated control. Loss in both leaves chemical degradation or sample loss as alternatives. Greater loss in the active preparation supports turnover when the comparison accounts for chemical instability and nonspecific losses such as adsorption. Measuring the identified product in the incubation strengthens the link to that transformation under the assay conditions. Under matched conditions, less parent at the same time or a shorter depletion half-life means faster disappearance. A higher reported $V_{\max}/K_m$ indicates greater low-concentration kinetic efficiency on the stated normalization basis.

**6. Was unchanged parent recovered in bile, and which excretion routes can be compared?** Start with Table 2. A single direct bile measurement can document the finding in that specimen, including in a study without a tracer or whole-body collection. A parent-specific measurement in directly collected bile supports biliary excretion of unchanged drug. Give the collection window, drainage coverage and denominator. Report an amount or dose share only when it can be quantified. Record detection alone in words. If only total radioactivity was measured, identify it as recovery of drug-related material. Compare route values side by side in Table 2 and keep intact and externally drained designs separate. Keep the three source totals separate and leave the unaccounted portion unassigned. Faecal label after IV dosing shows that drug-related material reached the gut. After oral dosing, unabsorbed drug can also contribute. Biliary recovery alone leaves re-entry into circulation open.

**7. Could protein binding explain a difference in total concentration or filtration? (optional)** Reuse Distribution Figure 4a and check the assay concentration range in Table 1. When total plasma concentrations differ in Figures 1 or 3, compare measured unbound concentrations in the same arms and times on the same dose basis. A smaller unbound contrast consistent with the binding measurements supports considering binding. Use a single fraction unbound in a filtration comparison only on its supported basis. Redistribution can also change early plasma shape and window exposure. Target-mediated disposition requires independent target-binding evidence.

**8. Did drug delivered in bile return to the circulation? (optional)** If the study compares intact and externally diverted bile flow, use Figure 1 for the matched plasma profiles, Table 2 for bile recovery and Table 3 for measured components. Bile diversion can also change initial oral absorption. An IV comparison helps separate this effect. Detecting dose-derived parent in a recipient after controlled transfer of donor bile provides direct evidence of re-entry under that design. A second plasma peak or faecal label alone leaves other explanations open.

**Additional readings**

- **Across-species assay comparison (optional) (Figure 5 and Table 4).** Compare direction only for compatible estimates or supported, separated two-sided ranges. Check each printed kinetic efficiency against the reported inputs on compatible units. If they disagree beyond rounding, retain the printed values and omit the affected ranking. Agreement across species can motivate a hypothesis about metabolism. Estimating whole-body clearance or the fraction converted by a particular pathway requires independent in vivo evidence.
- **Source-reported parameters (optional) (Figure 3 and Table 1).** Preserve each estimate’s dose, route, plasma matrix, method and phase. Distinguish an initial from a terminal half-life when the source does so. Consider volume and ongoing extravascular input when interpreting a terminal slope difference. A comparable IV profile and complete exposure help assess clearance. An estimate pooled across doses has no single-dose coordinate. Keep the dose range as context and send tissue-fluid half-lives to Distribution.

## Sanity checks and outlier handling

| What the reviewer sees | What to check or report |
| --- | --- |
| **Dose, source or baseline is uncertain** | Verify the actual dose, salt or parent-equivalent basis, route, infusion timing, matrix, assay and prior doses. If endogenous or residual signal cannot be separated from administered parent, retain the trace and defer a dose-attributed exposure. Avoid a systemic clearance interpretation after a local dose without supported systemic exposure. |
| **A value from an unnamed or microbiological assay** | If the method is unreported, retain the source label and record specificity as unknown. A microbiological assay measures antimicrobial activity, which can include active products. Show it as a separate trace and keep it out of unchanged recovery, $f_e$, renal clearance and the filtration check. Total radioactivity is neither parent nor a named metabolite. |
| **A blank, “—” or missing interval** | It is missing, not zero. Keep known amounts from other intervals and label the sum “over collected intervals”. Do not difference cumulative means from cohorts that change between times. |
| **Concentration without volume, or a below-limit result** | A concentration alone gives no urinary amount. For a representative below-limit collection with known volume, the assay limit times the volume gives an interval censoring threshold. Ultimate recovery still includes later or missing collections. |
| **A cumulative series falls, a value exceeds 100 %, or subtotals disagree** | Treat it as a data check, never as a test of a model. Keep printed values, record the discrepancy and set aside only the affected derived values. A later-day value expressed as % of that day’s dose can include carry-over, so the 100 % ceiling does not apply automatically. |
| **A printed total and its increments both exist** | Use one and reconcile them, never add them. Show both when they differ. Do not count an allocated cage wash twice or stack overlapping totals. |
| **Possible instability or loss to container surfaces** | Flag documented instability under the actual conditions. For a compound with sourced adsorption, low-protein samples at low concentration can read low. A justified lower limit survives a loss known to act only downward. A claim needing an upper limit also requires a bound on that loss. An unreported handling method is unknown. |
| **A published clearance implies less exposure than was observed** | Compare only a uniquely linked estimate for the same analyte, animal or group statistic, dose, route, matrix and occasion. Use dose divided by IV clearance or by the corresponding extravascular apparent clearance. For groups, use the published full AUC directly or verify that clearance was calculated from the same group AUC and dose. Dose divided by arithmetic mean individual clearance does not recover arithmetic mean AUC. If implied full exposure falls below observed AUC, check units, grid, boundaries and the original source before using the estimate. |
| **A point looks unusual, or a source check confirms an error** | Retain plausible extremes in the main display. Correct a verified transcription error from the source. Exclude a confirmed invalid result only from the affected calculation. If a plausible point drives the result, report the conclusion with and without that observation wherever both analyses are assessable. Preserve the original value, animal or group, time, source cell, reason and effect on the conclusion. |

**When urine and plasma windows differ.** With complete urine and plasma covering only part of the interval, label the numerical ratio as a proxy. An upper clearance limit requires an upper urinary amount and a justified positive lower bound on plasma area. Incomplete urine with a supported lower amount and upper full-interval plasma area can give a lower limit. When both are incomplete with no independent bounds, show the two measurements separately. For a fully covered interval, mean urinary excretion rate is $\Delta A_e/(b-a)$ and mean plasma concentration is $AUC_p(a,b)/(b-a)$. Distinguish a ratio of group means from mean individual clearances. Check voiding at each boundary, urinary delay, stable assay recovery and matching body-weight units. One mL/min/kg equals 0.06 L/h/kg.

**Does one collection determine the filtration conclusion?** Record the uncertainty in urinary amount, plasma area and physiology on the same analysis unit. Formal contradictions must survive supported timing uncertainty and prespecified widening. Use justified joint bounds or a statistical procedure covering the full search. A matched source assay coefficient of variation converted to joint bounds over every searched observation (Bonferroni), or replicate-based bounds with stated coverage, count as justified joint bounds. Pointwise intervals, unconverted assay precision or an assumed project tolerance support a sensitivity reading only. For each formal check, remove every eligible observation in turn and repeat the full calculation on the rest. Report whether the contradiction persists, disappears in an assessable rerun, or the remaining data cannot test it. An unmatched literature GFR also supports a sensitivity reading only. Withhold formal exclusion when required independent support is unavailable.

**Ranked hypotheses for Modeling.** For each study and species, rank the viable hypotheses from strongest to weakest support. Keep candidates with only indirect support, untested candidates and candidates with contrary evidence in that order. Record formally excluded combinations with their assumptions and evidence separately from the viable list. A contradiction supported only at sensitivity level moves a candidate below the other candidates with the same level of support, ahead of the next level; it does not exclude the candidate.

**Models to compare in this module.** If normalized exposures agree, consider linear elimination over the observed range. Use a supported dose or occasion effect to prioritize alternatives, and state which observation distinguishes them. For modeling, retain each finding on its measured basis. Link these records to the observations relevant to saturable elimination, binding, time dependence or recycling. Pass bounds with their direction and label estimates based on partial coverage.

## When other evidence adds to the answer

**Clearance sensitivity limits (Figure 1, Table 1).** When constant clearance and the dose basis support a clearance interpretation, a justified positive lower bound $L$ on true exposure gives $CL\le D_{\rm high}/L$ after IV dosing, or an upper bound on $CL/F$ after extravascular dosing. With nonlinear or time-varying disposition, label the target as the dose divided by complete exposure for that dose. For central IV parent after completed input with a supported drug-free initial state, a positive sum of decaying exponentials implies a conditional tail area of at least $C_{\rm last}/(-s)$ for a supported negative final log-chord slope $s$. Require an informative upstream shape check compatible with that model and propagate uncertainty jointly. Include only non-overlapping area and label the result conditional. Conversion to another clearance basis requires a supported positive lower bound on a constant unbound fraction or blood-to-plasma ratio, as appropriate.

**Nonrenal clearance (Tables 1 and 2).** With a supported upper limit U on CL and a lower limit $L_f$ on $f_e$ for the same target and analysis unit, $CL_{nr}\le U\,(1-L_f)$. It is never labelled hepatic or metabolic and never uses EV CL/F.

**Across species (Table 4).** Figure 3 preserves the source definition of each reported parameter. Add each species’ observed exposure, unchanged urinary recovery, interval renal clearance, as well as the largest measured component, largest identified product and comparable in vitro readouts where available to the existing species table (Table 4). A numerical comparison needs the same quantity, basis, dose, route, occasion and actual window. Keep incompatible rows visible with the reason. For each species, identify the one observation and one missing measurement that matter most.

**Plasma metabolite and radioactivity records.** The current rules catalogue each series by analyte, matrix, method, units and source. Pass verified, analyte-specific paired parent and metabolite time courses or independent metabolite-dose data to Modeling, with their pairing and assay basis.
