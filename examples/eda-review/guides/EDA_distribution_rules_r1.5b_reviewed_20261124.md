# Distribution EDA r1.5b · Study review guide

## Main question

**What is the simplest disposition model compatible with the observed parent concentration–time data?**

For each study and species, examine the decline after intravenous (IV) delivery ends. Identify the measurements that support testing one compartment, peripheral exchange or another disposition process.

## Candidate models and what they mean

**Disposition models for comparison.** Start with a well-mixed central compartment. In the linear models below, parent drug enters and leaves the body through this compartment, and any peripheral compartments exchange drug reversibly with it. The equations give central parent concentration $C(t)$ at time $t$ after an ideal IV bolus of delivered dose $D$ into a drug-free system.

| Disposition model | Structure and parameters | What would distinguish it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **One compartment** | With clearance $CL$ and volume $V$, $C(t)=(D/V)\exp[-(CL/V)t]$. | After IV input ends, log concentration falls on one straight line. Early and late samples from the same animal can test that prediction. |
| **Two compartments** | Central and peripheral volumes $V_c$ and $V_p$ exchange drug through clearance $Q$. At steady state $V_{ss}=V_c+V_p$. After a bolus, $C(t)=A e^{-\alpha t}+B e^{-\beta t}$ with $\alpha>\beta$. | A faster early decline followed by a slower tail supports testing peripheral exchange. A small component, similar rates or late first sampling can leave only one slope visible. |
| **Three compartments** | Two peripheral volumes and two exchange clearances accompany $CL$ and $V_c$. After a bolus, $C(t)=A e^{-\alpha t}+B e^{-\beta t}+G e^{-\gamma t}$ with $\alpha>\beta>\gamma$. | Well-sampled changes in decline rate can justify comparing a second exchange process. The number of visible bends alone cannot establish the compartment count. |
| **Concentration-dependent elimination or binding** | A saturable elimination model adds a maximum elimination rate and a Michaelis constant. A binding model describes how the bound fraction varies with concentration. | Reproducible later steepening or separation across comparable IV doses motivates these candidates. Binding measurements over the observed concentration range help distinguish them. |

The coefficients $A$, $B$ and $G$ give the initial contributions of the exponential terms. Each rate constant can depend on both exchange and elimination. Once central IV input ends, a positive sum of exponentials has a semilog slope that stays constant or grows shallower.

| What needs distinguishing? | Candidate explanations | What in the observations helps? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A steep early fall followed by a shallow tail** | Peripheral exchange, incomplete mixing, a changing cohort, residual signal or an assay floor | Serial animals, early sampling sites, predose records and the quantification limit. |
| **Later steepening or a second rise** | Nonlinear disposition, continuing input, recirculation or a measurement problem | The times of the decisive samples relative to dosing, the identity of the measured analyte and independent binding or elimination data. |
| **Different curves across doses or conditions** | Delivery, sampling, concentration-dependent disposition or a condition effect | Dose-normalized concentrations at matched times and documented differences between arms. |
| **A shallow tail after extravascular dosing** | Slow systemic input (flip-flop kinetics) or slow disposition | A comparable IV profile supplies the reference for Absorption’s paired-route assessment. |

## What can we tell from the figures?

Use questions 1–3 for measured IV profiles. Use question 4 for comparable IV doses or IV study conditions. Read the Absorption comparison for a plasma contrast between extravascular arms. Questions 5–9 apply when the study reports the relevant measurements. For a study reporting parameters alone, use question 9.

**1. Could one compartment explain the IV decline?** In Figure 1a, locate the largest measured fall, read its size in the original units, and note the times of the two samples. A long infusion or a late first sample can hide a fast early phase. After IV delivery ends, a linear one-compartment model predicts a straight decline on the log scale in Figure 1b for central parent-drug measurements with a supported baseline and dosing history. A straight line compatible with the measurements and their uncertainty keeps that model plausible over the sampled window. Two points can trace a slope. Testing for a bend requires at least three distinct times. A reproducible steep early decline followed by a shallower tail warrants comparing a model with peripheral exchange.

**2. How well do the observations support a change in slope?** In Figure 1c, higher positive segments mean faster decline, a rate near zero means little change, and a negative segment means concentration rose. Under the stated central-input model, concentrations after completed IV input decline and the semilog slope stays constant or becomes shallower. A rise challenges the prediction of decline. Later steepening challenges the prediction about curvature. Report them separately. Trace the change to the original concentrations in Figures 1a and 1b. Two adjacent rates share one sample, so a single high or low value can move the two rates in opposite directions. Check an early sample against infusion end and sampling site, and a late sample against the assay limit. If the mean bends, give the number of animals that show the bend out of those sampled on both sides. Use an arithmetic mean curve to describe the group. Even with the same animals at every time, individual straight declines with different slopes can produce a curved mean. Report the number contributing and the handling of values below quantification at each time. A group mean with unclear composition or censoring supports a descriptive reading.

**3. Which late IV interval can serve as a reference for Absorption?** In Figures 1b and 1c, locate the candidate late interval with adequate sampling and a consistent decline. Give the arm, animal or cohort, interval start and end, observations and clock. If the interval is unresolved, name the limitation. Absorption uses these observations to select its eligible IV terminal fit and interpret the paired-route contrast.

**4. Do dose-normalized IV profiles differ across doses or conditions?** For comparable IV dose levels or study conditions, compare the raw curves in Figure 1b with the normalized curves in Figure 2a. Identify when the curves overlap, separate, cross or come back together. In Figure 2b, a ratio of 1 means equal concentration per unit dose at that time. Above 1, the named numerator arm is higher. Below 1, the reference arm is higher. Several ratios near the same value away from 1 suggest a difference in scale. A ratio that changes over time suggests a difference in shape. A single matched time gives the fold difference at that time only. If there are no eligible matched times, read the curves in Figure 2a and state why Figure 2b is unavailable. Before attributing a difference to dose or condition, check the actual dose, input schedule, sampling site, assay and animals in each arm. Dose proportionality requires dose-scaled input profiles, including comparable infusion durations. If several factors changed together, report the observed contrast and the co-occurring differences.

**5. Which tissues reach the highest concentrations, and how long is drug measurable? (optional)** In Figure 3a, compare tissues at a common time using compatible units and the same measurement basis. See whether the sampled tissue peak occurs later than the plasma or blood peak. A peak at the first or last sample leaves its timing uncertain. For each tissue, read the last quantified time alongside the assay limit and the end of sampling. A tissue sampled for longer has had more opportunity to show persistence.

**6. Does tissue exposure track plasma or blood? (optional)** Read the ratio in Figure 3b alongside both concentration traces in Figure 3a. If the ratio rises while both concentrations fall, tissue concentration is falling more slowly in proportional terms. If tissue concentration is still rising, the tissue peak may be delayed. If both concentrations fall and the ratio falls, the proportional decline is faster in tissue. Parallel late slopes and a roughly level ratio are compatible with a shared terminal decline over the observed window. Sparse observations or wide uncertainty leave both stability and change plausible. Check whether the circulating concentration is close to its assay limit, where its uncertainty can dominate the ratio.

For exposure over the whole sampled window, use the tissue-to-reference area ratio in Table 4 with the same start and end times. State whether the tissue measurement is total drug in homogenate or unbound drug in fluid. For concentration ratios, distinguish individual paired ratios, the mean of paired ratios and a ratio of group means. Formal comparisons across time or dosing occasions require uncertainty that reflects the estimator and the study's pairing. Otherwise describe the observed ratios. For homogenates, check perfusion and whether correcting for residual blood changes the comparison.

**7. Does repeated dosing change tissue and circulating concentrations to the same extent? (optional)** Figure 3c compares the later dose with the first dose *within each matrix* at the same post-dose time. Reuse an existing circulating later-dose/first-dose ratio when it uses the same arms, times and estimator. Read each ratio against 1, then compare the two ratios with their uncertainty. Similar increases in the two matrices are consistent with the tissue increase tracking circulation. A tissue ratio above 1 that exceeds the circulating ratio indicates greater relative accumulation in tissue when the difference is supported. If either ratio falls below 1, report which matrix shows a decrease. Check the dose, route, formulation, input duration and dosing interval before interpreting it. A change in route or formulation makes the observed difference a comparison of regimens.

**8. What do unbound measurements add to the exposure comparison? (optional)** In Figure 4a, check whether plasma fraction unbound changes across the concentrations actually reached and covered by the binding assay. Where dose-normalized total concentrations separate in Figure 2a, compare measured unbound concentrations from the same arms and times on the same dose basis. A smaller unbound difference may support a binding explanation when the relevant fraction-unbound measurements agree. Figure 4b addresses tissue exposure directly. Read the unbound tissue-to-plasma ratio against 1 for the stated common window or steady state. An uncertainty interval that crosses 1 leaves the direction unresolved. For an eligible equilibrium comparison, classify against the project band of 0.5–2 only when the full interval lies in one region.

**9. Do reported volumes or half-lives differ across doses or conditions? (optional)** Compare a series in Figure 5 using the same parameter definition, route, units and observation window, alongside the reported variability. Distinguish central, steady-state and terminal-phase volumes, and identify apparent volumes after extravascular dosing. An isolated estimate belongs in Table 3. Without the underlying profile, a reported volume or half-life alone leaves the number of compartments open.

**Additional readings**

- **Late IV exposure (optional).** When Figure 1b shows a well-supported change to a slower decline, Table 2 gives the percentage of the observed area under the curve after that time. Report the start and end of the observation window.
- **Delayed tissue equilibration (optional).** In the Figure 3a inset, follow the time arrows. Higher tissue concentrations later at the same circulating concentration are consistent with delayed equilibration.
- **Organ recovery chart (optional).** At each sampling time, compare the lower amounts in the measured organs as percentages of the same upper cumulative delivered dose. Each bar shows a lower bound on the recovered fraction using a common dose denominator. Comparing actual organ amounts requires comparable estimates and their uncertainty.
- **Extravascular volume bound (optional).** Table 3 gives an upper bound on $V_c/F$, the apparent central volume, based on the administered dose and a concentration attributable to it. Read the bound alongside that sample’s time.

## Sanity checks and outlier handling

| What the reviewer sees | What to check or report |
| --- | --- |
| **An early bend, rebound or flat tail** | Check source dose, actual times, sampling site, mixing, analyte, matrix, predose signal and prior doses. Review mixing if a decisive sample is within five minutes of IV input end. Flag a decisive last concentration below three times the known assay limit, or give the limit as unknown. |
| **An observation repeats or a mean changes composition** | Determine whether rows are assay replicates, separate animals or a duplicated record. Verify which animals contributed at each time. For linked repeat occasions, plot each occasion separately within animal, compare the readings and pass the linkage to Modeling. |
| **Uncertain error bars, digitized points or assay limits** | Verify axes, units, markers and dispersion against the source. Prespecify the error using matched assay precision, suitable replicates or a labeled project default, in that order. Account for sampling uncertainty in group means. Pass censored records and limits to Modeling. |
| **A point appears unusual or the source confirms an error** | Keep a plausible point in the primary profile. Correct a verified transcription, time or unit error from the source. Exclude a confirmed invalid measurement only from the calculation it affects. Record animal or cohort, occasion, time, original value and units, source location, reason and effect on the conclusion. |
| **A volume or tissue amount conflicts with a physical bound** | Hold an IV bound below the sourced lower plasma volume (or blood volume for whole blood) on a matched basis. Hold affected tissue comparisons if supported lower organ amounts exceed the upper delivered parent dose. Check dilution, tissue basis, organ weights, dose and sampling against the source. |

**Does the result depend on one observation?** The descriptive IV screen compares the decline rates of the earlier and later halves of the post-input samples and needs at least five quantified samples. Repeat it three ways, omitting the first quantified point, the last point and all low-confidence digitized points in separate runs. Give the remaining count and reading. For each formal IV check (single line or curvature), remove every eligible observation in turn and repeat the full calculation on the rest: redo the line-feasibility calculation or rescan all remaining triples. If an assessable rerun loses the contradiction, identify the decisive observation and retain the candidate. If required independent support becomes unassessable, report the full-data finding and withhold exclusion.

Formal contradictions must survive supported timing uncertainty and prespecified widening. Use justified joint bounds or a statistical procedure covering the full search. A matched source assay coefficient of variation converted to joint bounds over every searched observation (Bonferroni), or replicate-based bounds with stated coverage, count as justified joint bounds. State the coverage or error level of a statistical result. Pointwise intervals, unconverted assay precision or an assumed project tolerance support a sensitivity reading only.

## Answer the main question

Name the simplest plausible disposition model, the observation that warrants fitting an alternative and the main limitation.

| Conclusion | What to report |
| --- | --- |
| **The observations contradict a model under the stated assumptions** | Identify input history, initial state, measured signal, animal or cohort, window, decisive samples, margin and robustness. A failed single-line prediction excludes the tested one-compartment combination when the uncertainty and robustness checks support exclusion. Robust later steepening excludes combinations retaining the tested linear reversible structure. The curvature check alone addresses later steepening. A concentration rise has to be assessed separately with its own uncertainty and dosing history. |
| **The model remains possible** | Say whether the observations were compatible throughout the window or whether omitting a sample or widening the uncertainty changed the result. Give the figure and the interval where the alternative would differ. |
| **The study cannot decide** | Identify the missing early samples, uncertain dose history, group composition, assay specificity or unsupported uncertainty that prevents a decision. Report the direction and size of any conditional result. |

**Ranked hypotheses for Modeling.** For each study and species, rank the viable hypotheses from strongest to weakest support. Give each hypothesis with its supporting and opposing observations, figure or source record, analysis unit, time window, uncertainty and the next observation or fit that could distinguish it. Treat estimates derived from the same measurements as one source of evidence. Tie candidates when the data cannot distinguish them and use model simplicity to choose which tied candidate to fit first. Keep candidates with only indirect support, untested candidates and candidates with contrary evidence in that order. Record formally excluded combinations with their assumptions and evidence separately from the viable list. A contradiction supported only at sensitivity level moves a candidate below the other candidates with the same level of support, ahead of the next level; it does not exclude the candidate.

**Models to compare in this module.** Start with one compartment while it remains plausible. Reliable slowing warrants testing peripheral exchange. For robust later steepening, use dose and binding evidence to prioritize nonlinear candidates and pass elimination questions to Elimination/Metabolism. Target-mediated disposition requires independent binding evidence for the relevant target. Compare fits on the same records and dose histories with the same censoring treatment. Check residuals where the models differ and whether the data constrain the added parameters.

## When other evidence adds to the answer

**Central volume (Table 3).** Divide the delivered IV dose by the highest eligible central parent concentration after input ends to obtain the nominal upper bound on $V_c$. Use a well-mixed sample attributable to that dose. For the project's standard infusion calculation, use a qualified end-of-infusion sample. A later, well-mixed sample attributable to the verified completed infusion can give a looser mass-balance bound. The extravascular calculation (optional) uses the administered dose to bound $V_c/F$, where $F$ is systemic parent bioavailability. An individual conservative bound uses an upper dose limit and a positive lower concentration limit for the same animal, with a prespecified sample or limits accounting for sample selection. Unresolved carryover leaves a sensitivity result. A supported bound constrains a fit for the matching animal or cohort. At equal doses and a common time, a fixed cohort’s equally weighted arithmetic mean concentration bounds its harmonic-mean volume.

**Tissue exposure (Figure 3, Table 4).** A tissue-to-reference area ratio describes exposure over the stated window. Interpreting it as an equilibrium partition coefficient requires evidence about exchange and local elimination. Record any dose delivered directly to the tissue.

**Repeat dosing (Figure 3c).** With a drug-free start, identical dosing and linear, time-invariant disposition, residual parent from earlier doses can add to the concentration at a matched time. A lower concentration on the later occasion warrants checking the dosing history and which matrix shows the decrease.

**Organ recovery (Table 4).** Compare the supported lower amount of parent drug in distinct sampled organs with the upper cumulative delivered dose at the same time. Match organ concentration and weight within animal, or use a justified joint amount bound for the same cohort. A product of separate group means cannot establish that bound by itself. If the lower amount exceeds what was delivered, trace the organ weights, dilution and dose to the source before interpreting the tissue measurements. The chart of organ contributions (optional) uses these same values.

**Unbound exposure for pharmacodynamic analysis (Figure 4, Table 4).** Identify the site relevant to the endpoint and whether unbound concentrations were measured or derived. A conversion from total plasma concentration needs binding measurements applicable over the observed range. For 4b, use equilibrated concentrations during documented steady constant input, unbound exposure over the complete dosing interval at established repeated-dose steady state, or a common observed window with a sufficiently stable tissue-to-plasma ratio. If equilibration is unresolved, report the observed-window ratio in Table 4 as an exposure comparison. For a single time outside documented constant-input equilibrium, report an apparent value at that time. Total homogenate exposure and unbound exposure at the effect site answer different questions. Check probe recovery for tissue microdialysis.

**Across species.** Compare findings on the same route, measurement basis and observation window. Tell Translation which species supplied direct evidence and which mechanism remains a hypothesis in another species.
