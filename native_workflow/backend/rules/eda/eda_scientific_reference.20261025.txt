EDA: DATA, FIGURES, AND PURPOSE
English Review Draft with Drawing Specifications
Date: 2026-10-13 (America/Chicago)

MAIN OBJECTIVE AND SCOPE

Use animal PK and preclinical PK/PD evidence to support prospective human exposure prediction and assess potentially efficacious human doses/regimens, their feasibility, and uncertainty. EDA describes the observations, available analytical inputs, and interpretation limits supporting that chain.

The target drug has no human PK, PD, efficacy, or safety observations in this project setting. Human outputs are prospective scenarios; a known-drug benchmark with held-out human data is a separate evaluation setting. EDA cannot establish human efficacy, safety, or a clinically acceptable dose. Later feasibility assessments must identify the constraints actually supported and retain unresolved safety limitations.

Supported NCA results and permitted literature parameters may support later translation review without a mandatory compartmental fit. Displaying evidence does not qualify parameters, select a winning index, estimate a PD target, or validate human predictions.

Combination therapy is excluded. Covariate and downstream-method background is retained, but covariate analysis and allometric/parameter-weight plots are deferred. Reuse Data's definitions, provenance, and known gaps; this review does not repeat Coverage, Necessity, or cleaning. A gap limits the affected description, not automatically all available evidence.

This is a text review. It specifies future displays and permitted descriptive calculations; no data analysis, plotting, or workflow implementation is performed here. The numbered items use data -> drawing -> purpose/limits/main-objective link, followed by MP background and sources.

DESCRIPTIVE CALCULATIONS AND SHARED PROVENANCE

EDA may execute a descriptive formula when its definition is established, all required inputs and their relationships are available, and no new scientific assumption is needed. Preserve original values and units, the formula and its source/definition, input links, grouping, calculation scale/order, and missing/censored-value handling. Distinguish source-reported values from EDA-calculated values. These shared requirements apply throughout; local sections state only additional conditions.

Allowed preparation includes:
- Unit conversion and time alignment using documented factors and reference events; retain actual versus nominal time and the original origin. A change in analyte, matrix, salt/active-moiety basis, or total/free basis requires an established relation, not merely a unit label.
- Dose normalization using a known, nonzero, compatible dose and an explicit per-administration or period basis. Zero-dose controls remain unnormalized. Normalization alone does not establish dose proportionality.
- Established response transformations: for example, log10 of positive counts on a defined sample basis, an endpoint-minus-baseline response with a supported individual or group reference, or percentage remaining from a documented nonzero reference. Preserve the chosen response definition; group baselines do not create individual pairing. No arbitrary constant is added to zero or censored counts.
- Ratios from source-reported or permitted descriptive inputs, such as fAUC/MIC or an explicitly observed-peak/MIC ratio with matched strain MIC, compatible units, concentration basis, and window. Computing a quotient does not authorize integrating exposure or estimating an unsampled peak. Likewise, a documented amount or collection total may be calculated from complete compatible measurements without assuming missing collections are zero or predicting unobserved excretion.
- Maximum observed concentration within a defined profile and observation window, using verified quantitative measurements. State whether it is a maximum within a verified individual profile or the maximum of a defined group-summary series; neither estimates an unsampled true peak. Preserve sampling times and censoring, which may prevent an exact maximum for the complete profile. A maximum among quantified records is labeled as such. Use this quantity in a peak/MIC ratio only when it matches the established index definition and concentration basis.
- Counts, means/medians, SD, ranges, quantiles, and frequencies at the verified unit/group level. State the statistic and denominator. Sample SD requires at least two contributing values and a stated formula; unavailable SD is not zero. Biological SD describes verified biological units; technical-repeat SD describes repeatability and is labeled separately. Technical repeats do not increase biological n, and pools remain pools. State any within-unit aggregation and quantile convention.

Order of operations matters. Specify whether each transformation acts on records, experimental units, or existing summaries before aggregation. In general, mean(C_i/D_i) differs from mean(C_i)/mean(D_i), and mean(log10 C_i) differs from log10(mean C_i). Use the order required by the established quantity; retain source summaries without reconstructing unobserved individual values.

Censoring is carried through an established formula as a bound or interval when mathematically supported, preserving inequality direction and denominator conditions. It is not an exact point or an SD/CI. If inputs or the relation are insufficient, retain a status/table entry. EDA introduces no limit substitution, imputation, invented pairing, or silent exclusion; any substitution in an existing source output stays labeled. A quantifiable-only summary identifies that subset and the omitted statuses/counts; unknown denominators or uncertainty remain unknown.

Deferred analysis includes concentration-time AUC/AUMC integration, new integration of response-time curves, PK parameter estimation beyond the descriptive quantities explicitly allowed above, exposure reconstruction requiring interpolation, extrapolation or a model, terminal-slope selection, PD-target fitting, inferential uncertainty estimation, and human simulation. Estimating fT>MIC from incomplete concentration-time coverage, transferring binding between species, or selecting an IVIVE/renal-clearance model is not simple display arithmetic. Existing analytical outputs can be displayed with their provenance; new endpoint definitions or assumptions require separate analysis decisions.

DISPLAY OVERVIEW

Select displays for the question and available evidence, not one figure per data category. Reuse panels and annotations where possible.
- Animal PK main figures: items 01/02/03/04/06/12; dose comparisons in 05. They describe the animal exposure evidence for later translation inputs.
- PD effect range: item 08; add or use item 07 when response timing matters. They show how the observed effects relate to the requested effect.
- Exposure/index-response: item 10, supported by regimen, linkage, and MIC information in 09/11/15. They inform later index and target analysis.
- Conditional evidence: binding, stability, and urine in 13/14 only when relevant to the considered translation approach; population MIC distributions in 15 when weighted attainment is relevant.

Supporting tables often suffice:
- PK parameter evidence: exact parameter/units, species, route/regimen, analyte/matrix/basis, method, data level, source, uncertainty, and limitations. Preserve CL versus CL/F and Vss versus Vz/Vc. Parameter-only literature evidence does not require an invented curve. See B09 and MAIN.MP003/005, PK-REF/TRANSLATION-REF.
- Regimen and PK-PD correspondence: dose/window/interval, treatment duration, PK source, PD experiment, matched MIC, exposure provenance, and linkage/transfer assumptions. See 09/11/15.
- Existing PD targets: target/range, reported uncertainty, index/units, concentration basis/window, effect, endpoint/reference, strain/model context, method, and source. A reported target is not automatically applicable or independently verified. See B10, NEXT.MP012/PTA-REF.

Retain supplied tolerability, formulation, administration, or exposure constraints in the relevant evidence table or figure note, with their study context and source. These inform later feasibility review; absent constraints remain unknown, and animal findings do not establish human safety.

COMMON DRAWING CONVENTIONS

Example labels below illustrate syntax, not assigned values or units for unexamined data. Axes and biological definitions follow the actual dataset. Layout choices are project proposals; WEB01-WEB13 distinguish methodological support from published examples and access limitations.
- Label variables and units, with analyte/matrix/basis and endpoint/window in the axis or panel title. A log concentration axis retains concentration-unit ticks; an already log10-transformed response uses a linear axis. Signed response changes remain on a linear axis.
- Keep raw observations visible when available. Identify whether each mark represents an animal, sample, pool, or summary. Lines connecting observations are visual guides, not fitted or continuously observed trajectories. Missing/censored observations and dosing discontinuities must remain visible; break lines where joining them would imply unsupported information. Source model curves, if relevant and permitted, are separately labeled.
- Identify source SD/SE/CI versus EDA descriptive SD, the variability level, n, and scale. Preserve asymmetric bounds. A bound that cannot appear on a log axis remains available in the linear view/table, with its omission disclosed. No new fits, smoothers, inferential CIs, or model ribbons are part of these drawing instructions.
- Use consistent regimen colors and complementary shapes/line types. Facets identify study/species/matrix/strain contexts, not estimated covariate effects. Comparable panels use common limits where practical; label exceptions. Avoid dual axes for different biological quantities.
- Preserve zeros, inequality signs, and missingness categories. Log displays require positive quantitative values; concise per-figure status notes or labeled bound markers handle other records without invented positive offsets. Censoring markers are distinct from uncertainty bars.
- Shared figure notes or companion tables may hold common source references, unit/summary definitions, formulas, grouping, and censoring conventions. Each caption identifies the variables/panels, exceptions, important omissions, and interpretation limits needed to read that figure. Full provenance need not be repeated beneath every panel.
- If an image, caption, methods section, or table gives conflicting definitions, retain both statements and their exact locations. Do not silently harmonize them or pool the affected quantities as comparable until the conflict is resolved. Unaffected descriptive evidence remains usable. WEB11 provides a total/free-label example.

DETAILED DATA, DISPLAY, PURPOSE, AND OBJECTIVE LINKS

01 | CONCENTRATION-TIME OBSERVATIONS

Data and details
- Concentration, time, units, time origin, study and profile membership, species, dosing regimen, route, formulation, analyte, matrix, and total or unbound concentration basis.
- Actual sampling time versus nominal scheduled time. Preserve which time definition the prepared data use; do not silently replace one with the other.
- Original numerical observations versus digitized observations. For digitized data, retain the source figure, panel and curve, axis scale/transformation, units, extraction provenance, and any existing reconstruction review.
- Measured points versus a fitted or predicted source curve. A line digitized from a model is not newly measured concentration data.
- Reported assay method, measured matrix, calibration range, LLOQ/ULOQ, validation information, and reported stability, recovery, or adsorption concerns. Retain supplied dilution/reanalysis provenance and the applicable range for the final reported result. Use these as context; EDA does not certify the assay.

Figure or table
Observed concentration-time points on the scale suited to the question; add the complementary view when informative. Distinguish any permitted source-model curve from measurements.

Drawing specification
- X-axis: elapsed time relative to the documented origin, normally on a linear scale. Example label: Time after dose (h). For repeated input, state first dose or most recent dose; retain negative predose times when recorded. Minutes and hours must not share an unlabeled scale.
- Y-axis: measured concentration with analyte, matrix, basis, and unit; for example, Total [analyte] plasma concentration (mg/L). For admitted animal PK concentration-time evidence, plan linear and semilog views by default: absolute profile/peak/sampling on linear axes and relative decline/low concentrations on log10 axes. Reuse the same source observations and verified grouping. Zeros, BLQ and missing values do not cancel a usable semilog view: preserve them in source records and concise per-figure status notes; do not create status subplots; never add offsets, substitute a limit or bridge unsupported observations. If no positive concentration with a supported numeric time exists, document the semilog exception and retain the supported linear/table view. Use one time basis per main figure, preferring evidenced paper sampling times. Label the displayed time basis in plain English: nominal study-method sampling times, reported sampling schedule, or digitized figure times, as supported by the selected column and its evidence. A paper sampling schedule does not establish actual per-animal collection times. Do not print internal time-basis codes in captions or imply that a display choice finalizes the modeling time axis. Otherwise label the retained source/digitized basis without claiming paper verification. Keep pixel times and source cells in companion data, not duplicate plots. Offer a separate time check only for a recorded source conflict or an explicit request; do not add automatic offset analysis. This display choice does not reconcile modeling time. Each figure has one main panel, full X/Y labels, ticks and units, its own legend and figure index. Never combine different administration routes in one figure. Separate IT, IV and other routes; different doses within the same route may remain together. Each route-specific figure retains full axes, units, legend and index. Use readable, range-aware axis ticks before adding annotations. For time profiles, use finer X ticks near the beginning when early observations would otherwise be difficult to locate, with coarser ticks later; preserve the original linear time spacing and time origin. Choose readable Y ticks appropriate to the data range and declared scale, with light intermediate grid lines where useful. On log axes label ticks in the original concentration units and retain the log transform. Do not hard-code hour or concentration intervals across datasets. Keep ordinary observation markers uniform within each series; do not specially enlarge or hollow first points, add first-time callouts or automatically generate a zoom figure for them. Separate symbols required for actual observation status remain allowed. Do not shift times, add a time-zero observation, align series, round source coordinates or promote a later quantified point to replace a missing/censored earliest response. Preserve original values, status and source locations in the companion records and concise per-figure status notes. Categorical and exposure-index X axes do not receive early-time ticks. Use the host-normalized catalog. This default does not apply to categorical endpoints, MIC, dosing designs, in-vitro response or already-log CFU. A straight segment does not establish a terminal phase or model. Log-axis ticks retain concentration values and units; zeros and censored records follow the common conventions.
- Marks: observed points; thin connecting lines for verified serial units. Group-summary curves distinguish source-reported summaries from permitted EDA-calculated summaries and identify their error definition. Destructive or pooled observations follow item 02. Break connections at recorded missing or unresolved censored observations; do not bridge them as if measured.
- Panels/legend: split incompatible studies, species, routes, matrices, or analytes; distinguish comparable doses/regimens within a panel. Do not overlay every context merely to reduce figure count.
- Annotations: dose events as rug marks/arrows; infusion periods as labeled bands; relevant LLOQ and, where needed, ULOQ references on the displayed concentration basis. Distinguish assay ranges across panels/records, including applicable dilution factors already supported by the assay record. A valid dilution-reassayed concentration is quantitative and retains its provenance; an unresolved above-range result is censored. Requested windows and censoring status use items 03/04.
- Evidence: WEB04 Figure 1 and WEB11 Figure 1 show semilog PK profiles with dose symbols and group means/SD; WEB02 supports assay-limit definitions.

Purpose, limits, and main-objective link
Show the magnitude, shape, spread, and sampling range supporting later animal PK inputs to human prediction. Interpret early observations, finite windows, and late/input-history evidence against the requested endpoint. The sampled maximum need not capture the true peak; visual shape does not establish compartment count, mechanism, model adequacy, or parameter qualification.

Keywords: actual/nominal time; time origin; source figure/panel/curve; digitization; measured/fitted/predicted; assay range; LLOQ/ULOQ; dilution/reanalysis; observed peak.
MP basis: MAIN.MP001.required_inputs, conditional_inputs, data_checks; MAIN.MP004.required_inputs, conditional_inputs. Background: B01-B03.

02 | INDIVIDUAL, SPARSE, DESTRUCTIVE, POOLED, AND SUMMARY DATA

Data and details
- Verified experimental-unit identity and membership in a study, group, regimen, and time point. A source record identifier is not automatically an animal identifier.
- Serial sampling, destructive/terminal sampling, sparse sampling, or a mixture. Sparse sampling does not by itself determine whether records are longitudinal or destructive.
- Individual observations, pooled samples, or group summaries. Retain arithmetic mean, geometric mean, or median exactly as reported.
- Contributing sample counts and independent biological units, where known. Report animal, tissue/sample, and technical-repeat counts separately. Four thighs do not automatically represent four independent animals; retain nesting within animals and the confirmed experimental unit. For in vitro studies, distinguish independent runs from samples or assay repeats within a run.
- Reported SD, SE, or CI and the quantity it describes; retain supplied variance/covariance information. Missing dispersion is distinct from missing observations.

Figure or table
Reuse item 01 with marks appropriate to serial, destructive, pooled, or summary data.

Drawing specification
- X-axis and Y-axis: reuse the time and concentration axes from item 01; data structure changes the marks, not the biological variable.
- Serial observations: one line per verified animal within its experiment; use small multiples if overlap obscures trajectories. Sparse serial records remain serial, but gaps must remain visible.
- Destructive sampling: sample points at observed times, without animal-level lines; optional labeled group summaries. Compatible time-point summaries from the same study/regimen may be connected as a descriptive group series, explicitly not an individual trajectory. Disclosed horizontal displacement may separate overlapping points. Nominal-time grouping or display displacement does not overwrite actual times.
- Pooled data: one symbol per measured pool, labeled as pooled. Summary-only data: one symbol per supplied group/time summary; show its reported n and error definition in the caption or companion table. Do not generate pseudo-individual points or distribution shapes from a mean and SD.
- Evidence: MAIN.MP001/004 supports distinguishing individual and summary evidence.

Purpose, limits, and main-objective link
Identify whose data each curve represents so later translation preserves the correct exposure and variability level. Group-mean curves do not supply individual trajectories or animal IIV; their peak is not the mean of individual peaks. Unknown uncertainty remains unknown. Sampling sufficiency depends on the intended endpoint, not a universal animal or row count.

Keywords: experimental unit; serial/sparse/destructive/mixed; pooled; biological/technical replicate; arithmetic/geometric mean; median; SD/SE/CI; variance/covariance; IIV.
MP basis: MAIN.MP001.required_inputs and conditional_inputs; MAIN.MP004.conditional_inputs and data_checks; NEXT.MP012.conditional_inputs and data_checks. Background: B01.

03 | QUANTIFICATION LIMITS, MISSINGNESS, UNSAMPLED TIMES, AND UNAVAILABLE ENDPOINTS

Data and details
- Original observation flags, associated unit/group and time, recorded reason, and applicable LLOQ/ULOQ or microbial detection/counting limits where supplied.
- Separate BLQ, unresolved above-ULOQ, genuinely quantified zero, not sampled, technical failure, and an endpoint unavailable after death or early termination. A stored numeric zero may be a placeholder and is not automatically a measured zero.
- For initially above-range samples, retain the final reported result and supplied dilution/reanalysis history. A valid quantitative result after appropriate dilution is distinct from a result that remains above range or unresolved; EDA does not perform assay reanalysis or certify dilution validity.
- Position along the curve: before the first quantifiable sample, between quantifiable observations, or late in the course.
- Planned sampling information, if available, and counts of records versus independent units. Unknown planned sampling is not evidence that a particular time was scheduled and missed.

Figure or table
Annotate the main plot; add a categorical observation map only when flags are otherwise unclear.

Drawing specification
- Per-figure status note and companion source table: retain BLQ, unresolved above-ULOQ, not sampled, technical failure and other recorded categories with their source record IDs and limits. Do not add a status band, strip or subplot. A genuinely quantified zero belongs on the quantitative linear panel; an unexplained stored zero retains its unresolved status.
- Optional observation map: X = time (numeric actual time, or explicitly labeled nominal occasions); Y = verified animal/pool/group ID, categorical. If destructive sampling leaves one record per animal, group rows by time/regimen or use a labeled group-level map. Do not invent a longitudinal animal identity.
- Marks: categorical colored/shaped cells or symbols, with a complete legend. If nominal-time cells summarize several records, state the aggregation and counts; mixed statuses must not be collapsed into one apparently pure category.
- Unscheduled/unknown slots: a blank is not automatically missing or not sampled. Show planned-but-missing slots only when the schedule supports that distinction. No continuous color gradient, error bars, or connected lines are needed for this categorical map.
- Evidence: WEB02 supports assay-range and dilution definitions; microbial endpoint limits require the experiment's own source.

Purpose, limits, and main-objective link
Show where unavailable or censored observations restrict an exposure endpoint or response contrast relevant to translation and target evidence. The map does not identify a missingness mechanism or justify imputation. Sampling proportions require a supported denominator; unaffected observations remain usable for their supported descriptions.

Keywords: BLQ; above-ULOQ; dilution/reanalysis; LLOQ versus microbial detection limit; censoring; observed versus placeholder zero; not sampled; technical failure; unavailable endpoint; planned-sample denominator.
MP basis: MAIN.MP001.conditional_inputs and data_checks; MAIN.MP002.required_inputs and data_checks. Additional project context: prior EDA PKPD09/17 and topic T14. Background: B01/B03.

04 | REQUESTED EXPOSURE WINDOWS AND LATE-TIME OBSERVATIONS

Data and details
- The exact requested endpoint, exposure window, and time origin. Distinguish a finite window, a dosing interval (tau), and exposure extrapolated to infinity.
- Observed window boundaries, internal unsampled intervals, the first and last quantifiable observations, and late BLQ records.
- Whether a boundary is observed, estimated/reported by the source, assumed by the source, or unobserved. Preserve distinctions among interpolation, back-extrapolation, and forward extrapolation in existing source information.
- IV bolus, infusion, or extravascular input; infusion end, subsequent doses, and relevant absorption information. For repeated dosing, retain predose measurements and the established time reference.
- When MRT or Vss is a downstream objective, retain the additional moment/input-history context; AUC coverage alone does not establish AUMC coverage.

Figure or table
Annotate item 01 with the requested window and input events; add a late-time zoom only when it contributes information.

Drawing specification
- X-axis/Y-axis: the same time origin/unit and concentration definition as item 01. Mark a finite requested interval with two vertical boundaries and a light background band. For an infinity endpoint, label the last observation and the unobserved tail in text; do not place an infinity tick or extrapolated curve.
- Window annotations: label the requested start/end, first/last quantifiable samples, relevant input events, and known unsampled gaps. Background shading marks a time window; it is not a filled area-under-the-curve calculation.
- Optional late-time panel: restrict X to the displayed late interval, explicitly label its limits, and use a log concentration Y-axis only for positive quantifiable values. Retain BLQ status in per-figure notes and companion source records and show infusion end or later doses that occur in this interval.
- Marks: preserve the same points and identity rules as the main plot. Do not select and color a putative terminal regression subset, fit a slope, or label the visually late interval as an established elimination phase.
- Evidence: WEB09 distinguishes observed-window and extrapolated endpoint quantities.

Purpose, limits, and main-objective link
Show the observations supporting the endpoint needed for translation or index analysis. Finite-window and terminal/moment-based endpoints have different dependencies. Retain the requested interval rather than replacing it with 0-24 h, tau, infinity, or a shorter interval by default. Late points do not establish a terminal phase; lambda_z selection/regression, half-life, AUC/AUMC tails, and extrapolation acceptance remain downstream.

Keywords: finite window; AUCtau; AUCinf; AUMC; MRT; first/last quantifiable; internal gap; bolus boundary; infusion end; predose; absorption-limited decline.
MP basis: MAIN.MP001.data_checks; MAIN.MP002; MAIN.MP003.conditional_inputs; PK-REF.MP001/002.analysis_method_requirements and MP003. Background: B02/B09.

05 | MULTIPLE DOSE LEVELS AND DOSE COMPARISONS

Data and details
- Explicit dose for each profile, dose unit and basis, route, formulation, dosing schedule, concentration basis, and sampling times.
- Salt versus free base; active moiety; mass versus activity units; mg per animal versus mg/kg; administered mixture versus the components measured by the assay.
- Whether the measured analyte is the administered compound, a metabolite, or a product formed from a prodrug. Retain any approved analyte/dose-basis conversion and its source. Routine unit conversion under the confirmed EDA boundary does not authorize inventing a new active-moiety, prodrug, or analyte conversion.
- Differences in study, regimen, time origin, matrix, or analyte that affect comparability. Dose normalization needs an interpretable denominator for the relevant profile.
- For an exposure-dose display, an existing permitted exposure endpoint or a maximum observed concentration calculated under the shared descriptive rule. Retain its exact definition, units, window, source/method, record level, and available uncertainty. Concentration records alone do not supply an integrated exposure endpoint.

Figure or table
Reuse dose-labeled PK panels. Add dose-normalized profiles or permitted exposure/observed-maximum values versus dose when the comparison is relevant.

Drawing specification
- Dose-labeled PK panels: X = time; Y = concentration, as in item 01; color identifies dose and the legend states its exact unit and basis. Different intervals or infusion durations must remain identifiable.
- Conditional dose-normalized panel (mandatory applicability assessment; see accounting below): X = time; Y = concentration/dose, for example (mg/L)/(mg/kg). State whether dose is per administration or over a specified period. Apply record-level normalization before grouping when that defines the intended quantity. Transform LLOQ/ULOQ bounds onto the same scale; raw concentration limits cannot be drawn unchanged on this axis.
- Optional exposure-dose panel: X = dose on its actual numeric scale and basis; Y = one defined quantity, for example an existing AUC over the specified window (mg*h/L) or maximum observed concentration (mg/L). Identify source-reported versus EDA-calculated values. Different quantities or windows have separate panels; do not combine AUC and peak concentration using dual axes.
- Scales/marks: choose linear or positive-valued log axes for the comparison, disclosing the scale. Retain zero-dose controls separately if a log X-axis is used. One point per supplied estimate or permitted descriptive value at its actual record level, optionally with descriptive summaries/SD across verified unit-level values. Keep reported estimation uncertainty distinct from between-unit descriptive spread. No fitted proportionality line, slope test, or model confidence band.
- Evidence: WEB09 supports exposure definitions and dimensions.

Purpose, limits, and main-objective link
Describe how profiles or existing exposures vary over studied doses and inform later review of dose-scaling assumptions for human scenarios. Similar normalized curves do not prove proportionality, linear disposition, or saturation. Extensions beyond the observed range require later evaluation; normalization cannot resolve incompatible analytes, matrices, or regimens.

Keywords: dose basis; salt/base; active moiety; IU/activity units; mg/animal; mg/kg; measured component; parent/prodrug/metabolite; dose normalization; exposure-dose; dose dependence.
MP basis: MAIN.MP001.data_checks; MAIN.MP003.required_inputs, conditional_inputs and data_checks; MAIN.MP004.conditional_inputs; PK-REF.MP003.analysis_method_requirements. Background: B02/B04/B09.


MANDATORY DOSE-COMPARISON ACCOUNTING
Every admitted concentration dataset must appear in an explicit dose-normalization applicability ledger. For documented comparable multiple positive doses, render an implemented source-bound C(t)/Dose view in addition to raw concentration views. If it cannot be rendered, record the affected datasets and the specific missing denominator, dose chemical basis, regimen/time relationship, comparability, upstream permission or implementation. A generic PARTIAL status or raw linear/semilog figure does not discharge this requirement. Unknown dose count is unresolved, not single-dose/not-applicable. Preserve source means as means, record-level operands, units, formula, order, source hashes, censoring and omissions. Do not silently convert salt/base, infer individual doses, replace BLQ, compute AUC, or equate curve overlap with proven linearity. Registered dose calculations have a separate result ledger; source coordinates and raw display validation remain unchanged.

06 | REPEATED DOSING AND INPUT HISTORY

Data and details
- Dose dates/times, intervals, amounts, sampling times, and known departures from the scheduled regimen.
- Time since the first dose versus time since the most recent dose. Preserve actual versus nominal timing.
- Infusion start/end and whether delivery was constant, varying, or interrupted where recorded.
- Predose or trough definitions, treatment day/occasion, and any independently reported evidence of steady state. A scheduled predose sample is not automatically a verified trough at the intended time.

Figure or table
Reuse the PK figure by day/occasion; a trough panel or schedule table is optional when it clarifies repeated input.

Drawing specification
- Whole-course view: X = elapsed time since the first dose; Y = measured concentration. Show each known dose event and infusion start/end on that timeline.
- Occasion panels: X = time since the documented dose for that occasion; Y = the same concentration quantity. Facet by day/occasion using common limits where meaningful. Do not superimpose repeated occasions without retaining their identity.
- Optional trough panel: X = treatment day or actual occasion time; Y = measured, timing-verified trough concentration in the stated matrix/basis/unit. Individual lines connect only the same animal; compatible group summaries may form a labeled descriptive series under item 02. A mistimed predose sample is labeled predose at its actual time, not relabeled as a true trough.
- Legend/caption: identify regimen, loading versus maintenance input if present, and actual versus nominal timing. Reported steady-state evidence may be noted with its source, but no steady-state threshold or accumulation curve is generated here.
- Evidence: time/input distinctions follow MAIN.MP001-003 and WEB09.

Purpose, limits, and main-objective link
Relate observations to input history so animal exposure and later human regimen scenarios retain comparable timing. Repeated dosing alone does not establish steady state, and changing concentrations need not reflect accumulation. A timing description does not estimate between-occasion variability or require repeated-dose data for every PK endpoint.

Keywords: predose; trough; tau; dose history; occasion; steady state; constant/varying/interrupted infusion; actual/nominal timing.
MP basis: MAIN.MP001.data_checks; MAIN.MP002.required_inputs; MAIN.MP003.conditional_inputs; PK-REF.MP003.analysis_method_requirements. Background: B02/B07/B09.

07 | LONGITUDINAL PD AND INFECTION-MODEL OBSERVATIONS

Data and details
- Measured response, units, observation times, treatment regimen, experimental unit/group, baseline, control, and response definition.
- For infection models: organism and strain, inoculum, infection site, host immune status, time from infection to treatment start, treatment duration, and untreated growth-control observations where supplied.
- The response reference: absolute burden, change from treatment start, difference from a concurrent untreated control, or a distinct outcome such as survival. Keep different endpoints and treatment durations distinct.
- Known assay/detection limits, observed killing/regrowth, follow-up after withdrawal, and recorded reasons for endpoint unavailability. Retain the animal, group, or independent in vitro run represented by each series.

Figure or table
Plot response versus time when actual time-course data address the question. For a fixed-endpoint question, prioritize item 08.

Drawing specification
- X-axis: elapsed time from infection or treatment start, using the supplied origin and unit. Mark treatment start/stop; do not reset infection time to treatment time without a documented conversion.
- Y-axis: the actual response. Examples: Bacterial burden (log10 CFU/thigh), Bacterial burden (log10 CFU/g), Bacterial density (log10 CFU/mL) for an in vitro system, or Change from treatment-start burden (log10 CFU/[basis]). An already log10-transformed value uses a linear axis; signed change values include negative values. Keep per-organ, per-gram, and per-volume bases distinct.
- Marks: verified serial-unit points/lines, destructive-sampling points, or labeled group summaries. Keep untreated control visible; distinguish regimen and facet by strain, site, experiment, or incompatible endpoint.
- Reference lines: a supplied microbial detection limit applies to absolute burden on its matching scale. It cannot automatically become one common threshold on a change-from-baseline scale. Carry response censoring into the display using the bound/status rules in item 08. Mark unavailable-after-death observations separately; never place them at zero CFU.
- Survival-only data: retain supplied alive/total counts in a time table, or source-reported survival proportions versus follow-up time on a linear 0-1 or 0-100% axis in a separate panel. A simple proportion may be calculated from complete known-status counts with its denominator/formula recorded; it is not a censoring-adjusted survival estimate. State denominators and censoring definitions; do not calculate a new Kaplan-Meier curve or infer survival proportions from incomplete event records.
- Evidence: WEB05 supports preserving response endpoint and duration. WEB13 Figure 1 shows strain-specific time-kill panels with growth controls and counting/quantification-limit context; late regrowth makes the time course informative beyond a final endpoint.

Purpose, limits, and main-objective link
Show the timing, duration, and range of observed effects relevant to the preclinical response a later human regimen aims to support. Preserve untreated trajectories and experimental context. Regrowth alone does not establish resistance; persistence does not identify a mechanism. Missing baseline/control information limits its corresponding contrast. Endpoint-only data cannot supply an invented longitudinal curve.

BACKGROUND KNOWLEDGE FOR READING AND INTERPRETATION

B01 | Experimental unit, aggregation, and uncertainty
An individual trajectory requires verified longitudinal identity. Terminal samples from different animals describe the groups observed at each time. A pooled sample represents the pool, not its individual contributors. Digitization identifies the extraction route, not the biological unit or aggregation level.
Arithmetic means, geometric means, and medians are different summaries. SD describes dispersion, SE concerns precision of an estimate, and CI describes interval uncertainty for a defined quantity. Preserve the source definition and independent unit. Technical-repeat SD describes repeatability rather than between-animal variation; tissues from one animal are not automatically independent animals. Group-mean uncertainty is not an individual exposure distribution, and summary data cannot reconstruct individual records.
MP locators: MAIN.MP001.conditional_inputs/data_checks; MAIN.MP004.conditional_inputs; NEXT.MP012.conditional_inputs/data_checks.

B02 | Time origin, input history, and windows
Two records labeled 2 h may refer to different dosing events. Actual and nominal times, predose samples, bolus input, infusion end, repeated doses, and treatment start must retain their meanings. AUC over a finite window, AUC over tau, and AUC extrapolated to infinity refer to different quantities. Observed endpoints do not establish that the intervening course was sampled adequately. A missing boundary is not permission to assign a concentration.
MP locators: MAIN.MP001/002; MAIN.MP003.conditional_inputs; PK-REF.MP001/002.analysis_method_requirements.

B03 | Quantification and assay context
BLQ is not a measured zero. An unresolved above-ULOQ result is not an exact concentration at the upper limit. Missing, not sampled, technical failure, and unavailable response records remain distinct. Retain the reported calibration range, LLOQ/ULOQ or microbial detection/counting limits, assay matrix and method, supplied dilution/reanalysis history, and known stability/recovery/adsorption information. A valid dilution-reassayed quantitative result retains its provenance and is distinct from an unresolved censored record. The relevance of these details depends on where observations lie in the curve and what comparison is being made. A reported assay-validation status is a source fact; EDA does not independently validate the assay or infer a missingness mechanism.
MP locators: MAIN.MP001.conditional_inputs/data_checks; MAIN.MP002.required_inputs/data_checks. Project EDA context supplies endpoint-unavailability distinctions.

B04 | What was administered and what was measured
Dose and concentration may describe different chemical or biological entities. Retain salt/base, active-moiety and activity/mass definitions, multi-component assay coverage, parent versus formed analyte, total versus unbound concentration, and matrix mass/volume basis. Established conversions may be evaluated under the shared calculation rule, but chemical, matrix, or binding conversions require evidence beyond unit labels. Dose normalization alone does not resolve these differences.
MP locators: MAIN.MP001.data_checks and conditional_inputs; MAIN.MP003; MAIN.MP007.

B05 | Response, reference, and desired effect
Absolute burden, change from treatment-start baseline, difference from concurrent control, and survival are distinct endpoints. The observation time, treatment duration, strain, inoculum and host/experimental context delimit each response. A requested effect level is not an already known exposure target. CFU censoring is not proof of sterilization. Regrowth and persistent response provide descriptive patterns but do not identify resistance or another mechanism on their own.
MP locators: MAIN.MP009.conditional_inputs/data_checks; NEXT.MP012. Additional project context: prior EDA PKPD16/17 and MOD01/MOD02/MOD04.

B06 | Exposure-response relationships and comparisons
An individual observed pair, a regimen-level correspondence, and a model-derived exposure are different evidence relationships. Retain the actual links. Free/total basis, index units, window, threshold and MIC determine whether values are comparable. Within-panel pairing can support that panel's description even if common records for cross-index comparison are unavailable. Source-supported reported exposures can be useful without recreating the source PK analysis. A documented later model output is not automatically missing raw data.
Exposure, MIC, and response censoring remain visible throughout that relationship. An established formula may propagate a mathematically supported bound (item 10), but that bound remains distinct from an exact observation or statistical uncertainty. Preserve any source substitution as an explicit assumption.
MP locators: MAIN.MP009; NEXT.MP012.conditional_inputs, acceptable_alternatives and data_checks; INDEX-REF.MP009.reference_profile.

B07 | Covariates and cross-study differences: background only
Body weight, sex, species/strain, health or infection state, renal state, and study/batch can matter to interpretation. Factors that occur together can limit attribution to any one factor. Preserve known labels and unknowns without assuming healthy or typical animals.
For this review, there are no covariate distribution plots, correlation matrices, covariate-concentration/parameter plots, covariate screening, or effect estimation. Parameter-versus-body-weight plots and allometric fitting are also omitted. Keeping study/species/regimen labels visible identifies the observations; it does not constitute a covariate analysis.
MP locators: MAIN.MP003.conditional_inputs; MAIN.MP004.conditional_inputs; MAIN.MP005.conditional_inputs/data_checks. The earlier source audit also identifies FDA preliminary-data-examination guidance as methodological background, not an animal-project plotting mandate.

B08 | Binding, IVIVE, and urine: branch-specific meaning
Free concentration, fu, and bound fraction are not interchangeable inputs. An isolated binding value has a concentration, matrix and assay context; direct free-concentration observations may require no total-to-free conversion. Nonspecific binding/recovery information describes limitations of the measurement process.
For IVIVE, preserve system, units and protein/cell normalization. Stability, half-life and CLint describe different supplied outputs. Hepatic metabolic evidence is only the component represented by that system. B09 | Disposition parameters and translation: downstream background
Keep the exact definitions of CL, CL/F, Vss, Vz and Vc. Absolute and apparent parameters are not interchangeable. Unknown applicable bioavailability leaves the distinction between CL and CL/F unresolved. Parent/prodrug dose divided by formed-analyte exposure does not by itself establish that analyte's absolute clearance.
MRT and Vss require information beyond an exposure area alone. AUMC tail behavior requires separate consideration from AUC tail behavior. The familiar infusion-duration correction in the retained MP analysis reference assumes constant-rate input; interrupted or variable input needs its own later treatment. These concepts explain why input history and late observations matter; they are not EDA calculation instructions.
A parameter derived from a group-summary curve is a parameter of that curve, not automatically the mean of individual parameters. Reported CL/Vss candidates retain their method, source, units, route, dose/regimen, data level and uncertainty. A literature-only value is not independently recalculated or validated by displaying it. Animal NCA results do not require a compartmental fit merely to exist, and Vss does not require repeated steady-state dosing merely because of its name.
Cross-species background remains parameter-specific. Repeated publications, doses or curves from one species do not create extra species. Actual study body weights are distinct from textbook weights. Candidate inputs are not qualified parameters, and there is no universal species-count rule. Allometric and parameter-weight plots are outside the current scope.

B10 | PD targets and prospective human/attainment scenarios: downstream background
Keep exposure description, index comparison, target estimation, and use of a reported target as separate tasks. A target specifies an effect, endpoint/reference, index, units, concentration basis, window and experimental context. A target awaiting fitting is a future analytical output. Displaying the observed effect range does not estimate it. A supplied target does not always require raw response data solely to recreate its source experiment.
Human concentration-time outputs in this project's prospective setting are scenarios, not target-drug human observations. Retain scenario dose/basis, bolus versus infusion, interval, treatment horizon, concentration basis and intended population without inserting typical values. CL and Vss alone do not uniquely determine a multi-compartment curve or identify Vc. Animal variability and parameter SEs do not establish observed human IIV.
Per-MIC PTA and MIC-weighted attainment have different input needs. Weighted results require the defined organism population, bins, frequencies and denominator; per-MIC calculations need not have those weights. Between-unit variability, parameter uncertainty and scenario assumptions remain separate. A typical-exposure/target comparison is not a population probability, and PTA is not clinical cure probability.

EI descriptive interpretation and human handoff
EDA packages source-preserving figures, exact plotted rows and concise necessary explanations. Describe concrete observations, their scope and unresolved source/interpretation limits. Check comparative claims against exact rows; disclose uninspected images. Flag suspicious points without declaring them statistical outliers or changing data.
Model candidates, compartment count, ODE properties, error-model choice and fitting strategy belong to the downstream PK/PD branches. Do not include recommendations or a model shortlist in the EDA human summary.
Independent ER review precedes the exact-version human checkpoint. Only explicit human approval permits PK and PD branch handoff. Share authoritative rules and unresolved constraints; route only relevant evidence. Handoff approval is not model readiness or permission to bypass the branches' input qualification. Human/horse holdouts remain excluded from current animal/in-vitro branches.

Accepted heading presentation: Keep figure titles concise: identify the endpoint and study/strain; put drug and dose in the subtitle when they would crowd the heading. Wrap long titles and subtitles rather than clipping or dropping scientific context.

Time-source scope: Time conflicts are scoped to the current study, route and matrix. A blood/plasma sampling conflict does not trigger a tissue or ELF time-check figure; an SC-specific warning does not apply to IT. Recognize explicit matrix-specific source schedules when the existing source-time values match; never snap digitized points or invent times. Prefer that supported source time in the main figure; retain pixel time in immutable provenance, not a duplicate main plot. Generate an alternate-time diagnostic only for a conflict applicable to that series or an explicit request; small numeric differences alone are not a trigger. Remove unrelated warnings from figure captions but retain the original notes in source records.

Time-kill annotation: For time-kill plots, display the source-matched compound MIC with units and the static/dynamic exposure setting in the subtitle when explicitly supplied. Retain the original treatment concentrations and censoring; do not replace tiny control placeholders by zero, infer replicate counts, or substitute MIC-normalized coordinates. Remove exact repeated title phrases without dropping strain or endpoint identity. Bind the MIC and exposure annotation to the current source study; conflicting or absent evidence is not filled by inference.
