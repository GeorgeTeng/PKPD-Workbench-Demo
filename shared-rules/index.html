Plain-language edition · 26 November 2026. Review guide for the shared EDA rules used in DBG01 and DBG03. See the technical rule text and the machine rules for exact criteria and implementation references.

These rules apply to Absorption, Distribution and Elimination. The shared rule takes precedence if a section's rule differs.

The checks named here

  • Single-exponential check (T6): does the IV decline follow one straight line on a semi-log plot?
  • Speeding-up check (T7): after input has ended, does the decline ever get steeper? In a linear system with central input it can only slow down or stay the same.
  • Dose-proportional exposure (T3): is AUC per dose over a common window the same at every dose?
  • Dose-proportional concentration (T3p): is concentration per dose the same at matched times?
  • Filtration check (T10): can filtration alone (filtration rate × unbound fraction) explain renal clearance?

Every formal check ends in one of three results: cannot be judged (a premise or needed data is missing), not ruled out, or ruled out under the stated assumptions (only for the model and window tested).

1. When group averages are good enough for a firm conclusion

Guards against: ruling out a model from averages whose spread or make-up is unknown.

Needed: the kind of average, whether the same animals are followed over time, whether samples are independent (when the check needs it), a real spread from the source (SD, SE or interval), and a test that covers every comparison looked at (rule 4).

Result: anything missing → cannot be judged (the curve and a sensitivity reading are still shown). All known and no conflict → not ruled out. All known and a conflict → go on to rules 2 and 3.

Per check:

  • Single-exponential: only geometric means of the same animals, with spread on the log scale. Arithmetic means never rule it out. A change in sample count alone does not stop the check if the source still supports the fixed-cohort comparison.
  • Dose-proportional exposure: firm only if each time used different animals (not assumed when unstated). Use the small-sample t value instead of 1.96.
  • Filtration: never firm on group averages.
  • Only the project's default noise → cannot be judged; the number is a sensitivity reading.

Example: arithmetic means of 6 horses, no SD, curve looks bent → we say it looks bent; formally "cannot be judged".

2. Is the conflict carried by one point?

Guards against: ruling out a model because of one odd sample (wrong tube, misread, wrong time).

How: leave out one sample at a time and rerun the same check.

Result: conflict stays every time → it counts. Conflict disappears without one sample → not ruled out, naming that sample. Nothing can be left out, or too few data remain → not ruled out, saying why. Keep the full-data result and every rerun.

Per check: the single-exponential rerun needs 3 time points; dose-proportional concentration with one matched time cannot be rerun; dose-proportional exposure keeps its window; the filtration check leaves out a urine interval and a plasma sample separately.

Example: 8 IV samples don't fit one exponential; without the 2-h sample they do → "not ruled out; rests on the 2-h sample".

3. Is the conflict a near miss, or caused by uncertain times?

Guards against: a conflict that only just crosses the line, or that depends on sampling times that are not exact.

Near miss: always report how far the conflict is from the line. No safety margin of our own; a wider margin only if the study set and justified one in advance. Conflict gone with that margin → not ruled out, "borderline". The 5 % default of the dose-proportional concentration check is description only.

Uncertain times: if the paper shows the times are uncertain and it could matter, rerun over the possible times, keeping their order. Conflict stays → passed. Conflict gone or flips → not ruled out, "sensitive to timing" (stated-time result kept). Check no longer makes sense → cannot be judged.

Example: conflict at "about 1 h (0.75–1.25 h)" disappears at 0.75 h → "not ruled out, sensitive to timing".

4. Many comparisons at once

Guards against: finding a "significant" result just because many things were tested.

  • Fix in advance: scope, what is analysed, which tests belong together, how they combine, and the level (default 5 % two-sided for the whole family).
  • The family covers everything that could trigger the published conclusion, including all units considered when one was picked after seeing the data. Count each underlying interval once.
  • Dose-proportional concentration: family = dose pairs × matched times.
  • A study-level "ruled out" needs a rule fixed in advance: "at least one animal" needs study-wide control; "all animals" needs every animal to reject in the same direction, none dropped; "at least k" needs its own calibration.

5. Ranking candidate models

Guards against: dropping or promoting a model on weak or one-sided evidence.

Each candidate (a full model: input, disposition, starting state, what was measured) goes in one group: reference, supported, indirect support only, not tested, or evidence against (stays on the list).

  • Sensitivity-level conflict only → stays in its group; order changes only if the same observation separates the pair.
  • Formal conflict in some animals → "evidence against"; report required, assessed and conflicting counts.
  • Formal conflict in every required animal, same direction → ruled out for the study. A ruled-out benchmark may still be shown as a comparison fit.
  • Required animals are fixed in advance; one that cannot be assessed stays in the count; one the check does not apply to is not.
  • Direction follows what each model predicts. A reliable fast-then-slow decline can put peripheral exchange first. A late steepening argues against both one-compartment and linear multi-compartment models. A failed single-exponential check alone does not favour two compartments.
  • Within a group: more independent evidence first (one curve counts once), then the simpler model. Indistinguishable → tie.

Example: 3 of 4 animals formally conflict and 1 cannot be assessed → "evidence against", not ruled out.

6. How well each question is answered

The status says how far the data answer a question, not which way.

Status When
Answerable (可回答) Every required unit reaches the needed evidence level.
Partly answerable (部分可回答) Only some units or sub-questions, or only a conditional answer.
Insufficient evidence (证据不足) The question applies but nothing answers it yet.
Not applicable (不适用) The study design makes the question void.
  • Judge each result, then combine; row order does not matter.
  • A limitation is attached to the conclusion it limits (unknown bioavailability limits oral clearance, not the window AUC).
  • A fragile result (sensitivity only, one-point, removal check not possible, borderline, or conditional on an assumed label or cohort) is passed on with its reason and makes the question partly answerable at most.
  • If a speeding-up conflict could not be checked for one-point dependence, nothing may assume "no speeding-up" (such as extrapolating the tail). Exception: if only assay-error information is missing, the curve's descriptive screen is clean and the other premises hold, a separately labelled sensitivity estimate is allowed.
  • The page shows a short status, the finding and one sentence on the limit.

Example: urine recovery estimable but renal clearance not → partly answerable.

7. Checks that exist only in the code

Guards against: conclusions drawn from numbers no rule covers.

Numbers may be reported and described freely. A mechanism, ranking or formal result needs a named check. A registered descriptive screen gives indirect support at most. Registered:

  • Renal sign test (Elimination): description only (per-animal ratios, median, range, sources); never "tested" or "suggests secretion". The filtration check stays the formal one.
  • Re-comparing IV clearance reported by the paper (Elimination): ratio, interval, pairing and source, corrected with the other dose comparisons (rule 4); partly answerable at most; always shown next to any dose-proportionality result.
  • Peak-per-dose screen (Absorption): peak per dose below ½ or above 2× while AUC per dose is within 0.80–1.25; needs sampling around the peak; indirect support at most.
  • Dose patterns (Distribution): described by shape only. If dose and animal are confounded: show the comparison, name no mechanism.
  • 1.5-fold similar/different grading: deleted.
  • Dose-proportional concentration on IV group means: one shared result for all sections; check the means and spread (rule 1) and that dose groups are independent at each time.

8. Precision needed for a formal interval

Guards against: a "firm" interval built on an uncertainty we cannot trace.

Every uncertainty large enough to change the answer needs a source that fits this study. If a key one is missing → cannot be judged (a sensitivity reading may be shown).

  • Data read from figures: assay error, group spread and reading error are kept apart; reading error needs a basis in the figure. Only the default reading error → sensitivity only.
  • Assay CV: must match method, analyte, matrix and range; a validation limit is not an observed CV; prodrug and active drug are separate; do not count assay noise twice.
  • Replicates: record how many and whether precision is for one value or the mean.
  • Filtration rate and unbound fraction: must match the animals and window; a single value, or an unbound fraction of 1, is not error-free.

9. Flip-flop reading

Guards against: mistaking slow absorption for slow elimination.

Compare the terminal half-life after extravascular dosing with IV, for every usable pair of windows:

Ratio (extravascular / IV) Reading
below 0.80 Steeper: check windows and group comparability
0.80 to below 1.25 Approximately parallel: compatible with a common terminal process
1.25 to below 2.0 Moderately shallower: absorption-limited candidate
2.0 or more Markedly shallower: absorption-limited candidate
  • Unresolved only when window pairs disagree. No usable window on one side → cannot be assessed (say which side). A literature IV value or another oral curve is indirect support at most.
  • Unresolved, unassessable or indirect is not evidence against flip-flop.
  • Absorption owns this reading; every page and the modelling hand-off use the same one.

Example: ratios 1.20–1.30 span two readings → unresolved.

10. Order of the formal decision

Go in order; stop at the first step that decides.

  1. Premises not met → cannot be judged (the filtration check stops here if the filtration rate does not fit the animals).
  2. Basis for an interval or test missing (rules 1, 4, 8) → cannot be judged; sensitivity reading if possible.
  3. Full data: no conflict → not ruled out.
  4. One point (rule 2): carried by one point or not checkable → not ruled out.
  5. Near miss / timing (rule 3) → not ruled out (borderline or timing-sensitive), or cannot be judged.
  6. All passed → ruled out under the stated assumptions.

Formal and sensitivity results are kept apart.

Concentration before the dose: the single-exponential check → cannot be judged if the predose signal is unexplained (sensitivity reading allowed). The speeding-up check still works if the residue is from earlier central input into the same linear system and we still measure parent drug there. "Input has stopped" alone is not enough: after peripheral input ends, the central decline can still be getting steeper.

11. Where facts come from

Guards against: a check silently using a wrong default.

Order: run settings, per-animal column, table, then paper text (labelled "source text (fallback)"). A missing fact is reported and the formal result withheld. Facts: dose level, dose each animal received, IV input length (0 = bolus), and the clock of the reported times (start of dosing or end of infusion). A stated duration beats the word "injection". Known input length but unknown clock → results that depend on it are sensitivity only.

All sections read printed numbers with one reader that tidies minus signs, powers of ten, full-width digits and thin spaces. Anything still not a complete number is not read.

12. Premises before comparing groups

Guards against: comparing groups that are not on the same footing.

Check every group, not only the first: same kind of summary (a geometric mean needs a log-scale spread), convertible units, aligned times (or a stated timing bound), and stated sample dependence. Unknown or different → cannot be judged, sensitivity only, premise named. A bound resting on an untestable assumption is an illustration only.

13. Figures and plain words

These change what is drawn and written, never what is calculated. Both drawing routes (R and Python) follow them.

  1. One clock. Time axes read "Time after start of dosing (h)". Only if the clock cannot be fixed: "Reported time (h), input end unknown" or "Reported time during ongoing IV input (h)". One axis name per figure.

  2. IV input band. A known-length infusion is a light band from 0 to its end, legend "IV infusion 0–X h"; different lengths share one band to the longest ("by animal"). No band for a bolus or unknown length.

  3. Every sample drawn alike, used or not. Below-limit samples are open circles at the limit. Never split samples by count (no early/late halves).

  4. Notes about one sample sit on that sample; panel corners are for whole-panel readings.

  5. (Withdrawn 2026-11-25.) No sampling times printed by the points; the first sample after the input is named in the caption of the early close-up.

  6. Lines and axes say what they are, e.g. "AUC window 0.25–31 h", "C / Dose (ug/mL per mg/kg)".

  7. Later doses are shown, not mixed in. Dashed lines mark them ("later doses at 24, 48 h"). Group overviews: one line per animal over all doses, open symbols after the second dose, caption states the regimen. Single-animal figures and absorption readings use the first dosing interval only.

  8. PK words in figures. No test names, margins, item numbers or status codes; a short code in brackets only where needed for tracing. Results better read as numbers go in a table. Times are after start of dosing ("0.05 h (end of infusion + 1 min)").

  9. Title, question, reading.

    • Title: "Horse · IV · I002 (12.3 mg/kg, 2-min infusion) — plasma concentration vs time, semi-log". No codes or questions.
    • Subtitle: the question, e.g. "Is there a dip followed by a second rise?".
    • Caption: the reading in at most two plain sentences.

    Only standard PK marks are drawn (points, infusion band, later-dose lines, Tmax/Cmax, last measured sample, below-limit samples, terminal fit). Test workings are not drawn, and a figure that would only show them is not made. A missing input reads "Not supplied in this package: ...".

  10. Plain words first. Every first-read answer is two sentences: what the plot shows, then the modelling suggestion. Animal counts are fine; at most one other number, only if it changes a modelling choice. Codes, ratios and boilerplate go in a collapsed "Numbers behind this" block. Wording comes only from the tool's results, never from knowledge of the drug. No figures → one sentence why, never "0 figures". A one-point check is one sentence naming the animal. The recommended model comes first. Elimination shows each animal's terminal phase and the plasma profiles.

    Example: "All 6 horses show a fast early decline followed by a slower terminal phase. Start with a two-compartment model; a one-compartment model does not describe the early points."

14. Results shared between sections

Worked out once, used everywhere:

  • IV window after distribution: found by Distribution, reused by Absorption; times counted from the end of dosing (no double shift). Keeps its samples, refitted slope and half-life, or the reason none was found (too few points, too little power, slowing in every window, no window passes, steepening, or screen not run).
  • Early mixing: a sample within 5 minutes after the end of dosing (5 included).
  • Predose zero reported by the paper: kept as reported, never a measured value; anchors an area only where the section justifies it.
  • Question numbering: stable ids across sections, with guide and old engine numbers; guide questions without their own row are recorded.
