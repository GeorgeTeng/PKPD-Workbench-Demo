Summary

Data. 6 horses dosed by IV; concentrations were measured in plasma.

Absorption. This study has IV dosing only, so there is no absorption phase to describe.

Distribution. All 6 horses show a fast early decline followed by a slower terminal phase on the semi-log plot. Use at least two compartments: start with a two-compartment model with between-animal variability on clearance and the central volume, and choose the terminal window by eye on each profile. In 5 of 6 horses the decline still bends after the early points are dropped, so a three-compartment model is a candidate; test it against two. In 3 of 6 horses the last points fall slightly faster than a straight line, so nonlinear elimination or binding stays an untested alternative. A one-compartment model is kept only as the baseline fit, because the data do not formally rule it out.

Elimination. In all 6 horses after IV dosing the late points fall on a straight line on the semi-log plot, with a terminal half-life of about 1.4 h. Model elimination as one linear clearance from the central compartment, with between-animal variability on clearance. Only one dose level was given, so dose-dependent (nonlinear) clearance cannot be tested. There are no urine or metabolite data, so only total clearance can be estimated. As a check, the slowest phase of the fitted model should decline with a half-life of about 1.4 h.

Starting model. Start with a two-compartment model (at least two compartments; test three) and one linear clearance; compare it with simpler and richer models before keeping it.

Gaps. No unbound fraction in plasma; no urine data, so renal clearance cannot be separated.
